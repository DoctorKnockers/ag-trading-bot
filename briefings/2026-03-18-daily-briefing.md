# Daily Strategic Briefing — March 18, 2026

---

## MARKET REGIME CHECK

**Conditions are deteriorating across all three sectors relative to last week.** The 30-year fixed has surged to 6.12% (up 12bps WoW per Freddie Mac), driven by bond market volatility from the Iran conflict and oil prices pushing toward $100/bbl. The dominant force right now is **geopolitical risk repricing through the rates channel** — the Iran war that started February 28 has broken the "flight to safety" Treasury playbook, with the 10-year climbing from 3.96% to 4.20%+ rather than falling, because the inflationary impulse (energy costs, supply chain disruption) is overwhelming the growth scare. The Fed announces today at 2pm ET and will hold at 3.50–3.75%, but the dot plot revision is what matters — markets are now pricing only one cut this year, likely not before December.

---

## THESIS TRACKER

### Private Lending
**Status: Spreads compressing, but watch the credit box.**

National hard money averages sit at 8.5–11.2%, with Tier 1 borrowers accessing mid-8s at 1 point. The story here is institutional capital flooding into private credit — Moody's projects global private credit AUM exceeding $2 trillion in 2026, and RTL (Residential Transition Lending) is a growing slice. This capital influx is driving spread compression and fee transparency ("1 and 1" structures replacing 2-3 point origination norms). **However**, the risk is on the exit side: if flip margins stay compressed (see below) and STR revenues soften, borrower defaults will tick up. Some lenders are pushing leverage to 92.5% LTC — aggressive in a market where 17% of flippers are still selling below ARV. **Thesis: Capital inflows are mispricing tail risk on the credit side. Stay cautious on high-LTC paper.**

### Short-Term Rentals
**Status: Regulatory noose tightening; fundamentals stable but uninspiring.**

U.S. occupancy averaging 50–54% nationally with ADR at ~$173. Supply growth decelerating to sub-4% (healthy). The real story is regulatory: California's SB 346 (effective Jan 1) gives cities platform-level enforcement tools, Idaho's HB 583 pushes back with state preemption, and the EU mandates registration numbers by May 2026. NYC's Local Law 18 has effectively killed the sub-30-day arbitrage market there. Net-net: **the regulatory environment is bifurcating** — some states are protecting operators (Idaho, Florida at the state level), while cities and blue states are systematically building enforcement infrastructure. **Thesis: Revenue stability is masking a creeping regulatory compression of the investable universe. Operators need to be long in preemption states and short in cities with platform-reporting mandates.**

### Fix & Flip
**Status: Sentiment rising but margins remain historically thin. Distressed pipeline building.**

The Burns + Kiavi Fix & Flip Index jumped to 62 in Q4 2025 (from 56 in Q3), and 71% of flippers plan to buy more in 2026 — the most bullish reading in 4 years. But actual flipped home sales fell to 37,000 in Q4, down 9.2% YoY, the lowest since Q3 2015. Gross margins at 25.1% in mid-2025 were the worst since 2008. **The positive signal**: only 17% of flippers now report selling below ARV, down from 21% last quarter, suggesting the exit pricing environment is firming. Foreclosure filings up 5.8% in H1 2025 YoY, with Florida leading distressed inventory (1 in 2,182 housing units). SW Florida comps declining with inventory up 40% YoY. **Thesis: The setup is improving for disciplined operators — distressed supply is building while competition is dropping (lowest flip volumes in a decade). But the carry cost environment (insurance, taxes, rates) means your underwriting better be surgical. Midwest markets (Cleveland: 50%+ gross margins on a $55K–$95K basis to $145-160K ARV) are structurally more attractive than Florida right now.**

## OPPORTUNITIES & THREATS

### Opportunities

1. **Midwest fix-and-flip arbitrage.** While capital and attention remain fixated on Sun Belt markets, Cleveland/Indianapolis/Detroit offer 50%+ gross margins with lower insurance/tax drag. The distressed pipeline in Indiana (4th-highest foreclosure rate nationally) is under-competed relative to Florida. This is a classic "boring market" alpha play.

2. **Private lending in preemption states.** Idaho's HB 583 is a leading indicator — states are actively defending STR operators against local bans. Deploy bridge capital in these jurisdictions where the STR exit strategy remains viable and regulatory risk is being legislated away.

3. **Florida distressed acquisition (patient capital only).** The carrying-cost trifecta (insurance >$6K/yr, rising property taxes, 6%+ conventional rates) is creating forced sellers. If you can buy right and hold through the insurance cycle normalization, basis levels in Lakeland/Jacksonville are resetting meaningfully. But timing is uncertain — this is a 12-18 month thesis, not a quick flip.

### Threats

1. **Oil at $100/bbl is a second-order wrecking ball.** Beyond its direct effect on rates and inflation expectations, $100 oil hits renovation costs (materials transport), tourism-dependent STR markets (airfare sensitivity), and consumer confidence. If the Iran conflict escalates further and oil sustains above $100, core PCE re-acceleration above 3% will push the Fed further from cutting and potentially force them to discuss hikes. Two FOMC dissents in January were on the dovish side — watch if hawkish dissents emerge.

2. **The Warsh Fed.** Powell steps down May 15. Kevin Warsh's nomination signals a potentially more hawkish orientation. If confirmed, the forward guidance shift alone could push the 10-year toward 4.5%+ and reprice all levered real estate strategies. This is the biggest under-discussed risk in the sector.

3. **Platform-reporting enforcement in STR.** California's SB 346 is a template other states will copy. Once cities can pull address-level data from Airbnb/VRBO on demand, enforcement becomes automated. Unlicensed operators will get purged. If you're lending against STR income, verify the operator's licensing status — unlicensed cash flows are now structurally at risk.

## CONTRARIAN TAKE

**The consensus on RE Twitter/BiggerPockets is that rate cuts are imminent and you should "buy now before the spring rush."** This is wrong on both timing and logic.

The market is pricing exactly one cut in 2026, likely not before December. Core PCE has re-accelerated to 3.1%. Oil is at $100. The Fed is about to lose its most dovish chair in a generation and replace him with a known hawk. The conditions that would trigger meaningful rate relief — a deflationary recession or a resolution to the Iran conflict — are not base-case scenarios.

More importantly, the "buy before rates drop" thesis has causality backwards for operators. Lower rates will bring *more* competition (sidelined capital re-enters), compressing margins further. **The best time to be acquiring is now, while volumes are at decade lows and competition is thin** — not because rates will drop, but because you're buying at favorable basis levels with less bidding pressure. The margin of safety is in the entry price, not the rate environment. Every flipper waiting for 5% money is a flipper not bidding against you today.

## ONE NUMBER THAT MATTERS

### 37,000 — Flipped home sales in Q4 2025 (lowest since Q3 2015)

This is the most important signal for a cross-sector operator because it confirms a **supply-side clearing** in the fix-and-flip market that creates opportunity across all three verticals:

- **For flipping:** Lowest competition in a decade means better acquisition pricing and less bidding-war pressure at courthouse steps and MLS.
- **For private lending:** Fewer active flippers means the borrowers still in the market are more experienced and disciplined (adverse selection working in your favor for once). Origination volumes may be lower, but credit quality of the remaining pool is higher.
- **For STR:** Fewer flips completing means fewer new units entering the STR supply pipeline (many flippers were converting to STR as an exit strategy in 2023-2024). This naturally throttles supply growth in the STR market without any regulatory intervention.

When the least sophisticated operators exit, the playing field improves for everyone still standing. This number tells you: **the washout is well advanced.** The operators left are the ones who know how to underwrite in a tough market. Position accordingly.
